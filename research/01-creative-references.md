# Making the tangible intangible — Collection 1

The version of **“making the tangible intangible”** that interests me most is: **an object loses its physical form, but keeps its presence, identity, or emotional force.** A place becomes sound. A fruit becomes a scent you can transmit. A building becomes weather.

Working from your phrase, here’s an open-ended collection of connections—some recent, some older references too good to leave out. The connections to your concept are my interpretations.

1. **Osmo — a fruit becomes a file, then a smell.**
   This young technology company demonstrated a process in 2024 that analyzes a physical sample’s scent, translates it into data, and uses a formulation robot to recreate it. Its account describes an early experiment with coconut. The interesting leap is that **something can travel without the thing itself traveling**. Imagine sending the smell of someone’s kitchen instead of a photograph of it. [See the process and video](https://www.osmo.ai/blog/update-scent-teleportation-we-did-it).

2. **Coperni’s Air Swipe Bag — an accessory approaching disappearance.**
   The 2024 bag, developed with artist and researcher Ioannis Michaloudis, uses silica aerogel: approximately 99% air, weighing just 33 grams. It looks like a small cloud that has reluctantly agreed to become a handbag. What catches me is the tension between the recognizable outline of a possession and how little material remains. **How much can you remove before an object becomes only the idea of itself?** [See the bag](https://www.istitutomarangoni.com/en/maze35/design/the-coperni-can-revolution-whats-behind-the-99-air-swipe-bag).

3. **Jeppe Hein’s *Invisible Labyrinth* — walls become behavior.**
   Visitors navigate an apparently empty room wearing headsets that vibrate when they encounter infrared “walls.” There is no physical maze, yet people move as though there is. This is an unusually precise illustration of your phrase: **the structure disappears, but its power to organize bodies remains.** It’s from 2005, and still feels fertile for fashion, interfaces, choreography, or social spaces. [Explore the work](https://www.jeppehein.net/project_id.php?id=125&path=works).

4. **Cities and Memory — a place becomes its imagined double.**
   This ongoing sound project pairs recordings of real places with artistic reinterpretations of those recordings. You can move between a location’s actual sounds and what an artist hears in them. I like it as a model for transforming something without losing its origin: **a city becomes a mood, rhythm, or private association.** Start by finding a place you know and comparing the two versions. [Listen and explore](https://citiesandmemory.com/what-is-cities-and-memory-about/).

5. **Diller Scofidio + Renfro’s *Blur Building* — architecture becomes atmosphere.**
   For the 2002 Swiss Expo, the architects created a pavilion enveloped in fog generated from lake water. Visitors entered a whiteout where the usual visual coordinates of architecture dissolved. There was even a water bar: you could drink the material surrounding you. **A building becomes a condition you enter.** The photographs are a particularly strong visual reference. [See the project](https://dsrny.com/project/blur-building).

6. **Jana Winderen’s *Energy Field* — a landscape becomes listening.**
   Winderen recorded inside glacier crevasses, in fjords, and underwater, then layered these recordings into compositions. The 2010 album lets a vast physical environment arrive through tiny, intimate sounds. For me, it suggests **preserving a landscape through its activity and resonance**, rather than its image. Listen while deliberately avoiding pictures of the places. [Listen / find the album](https://www.janawinderen.com/releases/energy-field).

7. **Mary Ruefle’s erasure books — language becomes absence.**
   Ruefle covers much of the text in old books with correction fluid, paint, and collage, leaving fragments that become new poems. A selection appeared in *The Paris Review* in 2025. The pages remain physical, but their original certainty disappears into suggestion. **Subtraction creates a larger imaginative space.** This feels especially useful if your research touches editing, packaging, memory, or how little needs to be said. [Look through the pages](https://www.theparisreview.org/blog/2025/08/12/erasure-notebooks/).

8. **Holosonics’ Audio Spotlight — a room within a room, made of sound.**
   Its directional speakers use ultrasound to create narrow listening areas. You step into an audible zone and then out of it, without crossing a physical partition. This is an established company rather than a new startup, but the creative possibility is lovely: **intimacy and boundaries become something spatial yet invisible.** Imagine a gallery where each empty patch of floor holds a different voice. [See the technology and demonstrations](https://www.holosonics.com/).

9. **David Lowery’s *A Ghost Story* — a home becomes attachment.**
   The 2017 film follows a ghost returning to the house he shared with his partner, increasingly unmoored from ordinary time. My connection here is emotional: the tangible things of a shared life gradually give way to the attachment that outlasts them. **What remains of a place after the people and objects that defined it are gone?** [Watch the trailer](https://a24films.com/films/a-ghost-story).

**I’d start with the Air Swipe Bag, *Invisible Labyrinth*, and Cities and Memory.** Together they offer three very different ways into the concept: almost removing matter, replacing matter with sensation, and translating matter into imagination.
