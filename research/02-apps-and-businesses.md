# Making the tangible intangible — Collection 2: Apps and businesses

Researched September 18, 2026. A mix of recent launches and growing companies, rather than a claim that every company is newly founded or still small. Product descriptions are sourced below; conceptual connections and suggested applications are interpretations.

Your examples suggest an interest in technology that makes lived experience easier to remember, discover, and share. Corner turns geography into shared taste; Mantel brings scattered traces of a life together into memory.

## Your reference points

- **Corner:** A social map for saving and discovering places through people’s tastes and recommendations. [Explore its city guides](https://www.corner.inc/guides).
- **Mantel:** The likely match is Mantel at mantelmoments.com. Its platform combines personal media, including photos, locations, and calendars, and uses AI to compose memories for reflection and sharing. A founder announcement identifies Isabella Serrano Martinez and Aekta Shah van Dijk. The Brown affiliation was not independently verified. [Platform](https://www.mantelmoments.com/the-platform) · [Founder announcement](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/isabella-ism_almost-exactly-a-year-ago-i-decided-to-pursue-activity-7475305044309913600-7Et8).

## 1. Remento — a family member’s voice becomes an inheritance

People respond to prompts by recording stories. Speech-to-Story technology turns those recordings into readable narratives, and a printed book includes QR codes linking back to the original voice. Founded in 2021; its first book was sold in 2023.

**Who it serves:** Families who want to preserve stories but find writing a memoir overwhelming.

**Why it fits:** It preserves the person’s presence alongside the facts. The book is a doorway back to a voice. It moves in both directions between tangible and intangible.

[How it works](https://www.remento.co/how-it-works) · [Founding story](https://root.remento.co/our-story)

## 2. Cosmos — the things you notice become a portrait of your taste

An app for collecting images, videos, links, and notes into visual collections, with search by color and visual similarity. Founder Andy McCune describes building it with Luca Marra as a side project; his January 2026 essay discusses its growth and direction.

**Who it serves:** Designers, creative teams, and anyone accumulating references without a satisfying place to put them.

**Why it fits:** A chair, photograph, room, and album cover can collectively express a sensibility you would struggle to describe in words. Curation becomes a form of self-portraiture.

[Explore Cosmos](https://www.cosmos.so/) · [Founder’s essay](https://www.cosmos.so/blog/the-future-of-cosmos)

## 3. Whering — your wardrobe becomes a set of possibilities

Founded by Bianca Rangecroft, Whering lets people photograph or import clothing, assemble outfits, receive styling suggestions, plan packing, and explore friends’ wardrobes.

**Who it serves:** People who own plenty of clothes but struggle to see how to use them.

**Why it fits:** A cupboard of separate objects becomes a flexible system of combinations and self-expression. Technology can create a feeling of newness from things already owned. This is a growing app, not a just-launched microbusiness.

[Product](https://whering.co.uk/) · [Founder and mission](https://whering.co.uk/about)

## 4. Airbuds — listening becomes a quiet form of keeping in touch

A music-sharing widget that lets friends see one another’s listening activity and react to it.

**Who it serves:** Friends who want lightweight everyday connection and music discovery.

**Why it fits:** A listening habit becomes a small signal of someone’s day. My interpretation: it recaptures something of hearing music coming from a friend’s room, even when you live apart.

[Explore the app](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/airbuds-widget/id1638906106)

## 5. Soundmap — the record collection becomes a social game

Players walk around to collect songs at map locations, trade with others, and build collections with different rarity levels. The team says it designed the first version in January 2024.

**Who it serves:** Music fans who enjoy collecting, showing their taste, and meeting other fans.

**Why it fits:** It carries the rituals of collecting records—finding, trading, completing, displaying—into a digital experience tied to physical movement. These are in-game collectibles, not ownership of music rights.

[App and features](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/soundmap-music-collecting/id6476420863) · [Co-founder’s account](https://www.zibogao.com/soundmap) · [Team’s first-year retrospective](https://www.reddit.com/r/Soundmap/comments/1hr9uqb)

## 6. Timeleft — an ordinary restaurant table becomes a route to belonging

The service matches people for gatherings and handles venues and logistics. Its dinner format brings you together with five new people; the platform also lists drinks and runs.

**Who it serves:** Adults who want to meet people but lack a natural setting or social organizer.

**Why it fits:** The business creates the conditions for a relationship. The restaurant and meal are physical; the value people hope to leave with is connection. It is now an international business, included for the idea rather than its smallness.

[Explore Timeleft](https://timeleft.com/)

## 7. Polycam — a room becomes something you can send

Polycam offers tools for scanning objects and spaces into digital 3D captures, floor plans, and virtual walkthroughs.

**Who it serves:** Designers, builders, property teams, and creators who need to capture or communicate physical space.

**Why it fits:** This is one of the most literal examples of tangible becoming digital. A creative application would be scanning a childhood room before a move, preserving spatial relationships rather than only separate photographs. That is a suggested use, not a claim that Polycam specializes in family memory.

[Explore Polycam](https://poly.cam/)

## 8. mymind — a pile of saved things becomes a searchable memory

A private app for saving notes, bookmarks, articles, and images. It organizes saved material and supports associative retrieval by details such as color, keyword, brand, or date.

**Who it serves:** People whose ideas are scattered across screenshots, tabs, and notes.

**Why it fits:** It approaches memory through association: remembering a color or feeling can be enough to find a reference. It is particularly interesting beside Cosmos: one emphasizes a private personal archive, while the other also helps you discover other people’s visual worlds.

[Explore mymind](https://mymind.com/)

The closest matches to the examples you supplied are Remento, Cosmos, and Airbuds. Polycam is the strongest literal illustration of physical things becoming portable digital experiences; Timeleft is the useful adjacent case of technology creating an intangible human benefit in a physical setting.
